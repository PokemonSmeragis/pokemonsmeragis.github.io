Introduction

This page documents observable battle behavior: how the AI chooses moves and switches, how it handles KO opportunities, and how it reacts to revealed information.

AI profiles: Standard vs Boss

Standard Trainers

  • Core goal order: don't waste turns → take clean KOs → improve matchup.
  • Not always perfect information: some reads depend on what has been revealed during the battle.
  • Sensible switching and post-KO sends, with more room for surprise value.

Boss fights

  • Same tactical foundation as Standard Trainers.
  • More accurate reads of your overall team situation → fewer blind lines.
  • More coherent switching vs coverage; fewer free turns; cleaner risk management.

Turn decision pipeline

In practice, a turn follows this pipeline (simplified but faithful to observed behavior):

  1. Switch evaluationIf the matchup is clearly unfavorable and a better entry exists, switching becomes a primary line.
  2. Move filteringLow-impact or “dead” moves are de-prioritized (no hit, no value, wrong context).
  3. KO searchIf a reliable KO line exists this turn (including via priority), the AI tends to take it.
  4. Context scoringIf no KO is available, it weighs expected damage + situational utility (pivoting, denial, immediate setup).

KO logic, priority, and speed control

  • If slower but holding a priority move that secures the KO, it tends to select it.
  • If already faster and multiple moves KO, it may choose between them.
  • If the player has a fast kill and the AI has a slow kill, it will go for it anyway, unless a better matchup is on bench.
  • If the target is already in KO range: it tends to close rather than take slow lines.
  • If its own HP is low: it avoids overly greedy lines that lose the piece without compensation.
  • If it's healthy: it's more willing to take control lines (positioning + pressure).
  • In a speed tie, priority is not strictly forced if multiple lines are considered equivalent.

Competitive takeaway: once you're in KO range, expect fewer “free utility turns” — the AI often closes immediately.

Switching

The AI switches when the switch materially improves the next-turn outcome: key immunities/resists, reduced KO risk, and better pressure on re-entry.

Signals that increase switching

  • Imminent KO risk with a safer entry available.
  • A teammate provides a key immunity/resist versus the current threat.
  • Available moves apply too little pressure (low meaningful damage / low value).
  • After a KO, it often selects a sensible revenge/check instead of a random send.

Signals that reduce switching

  • No clearly better answer exists on the bench.
  • Staying enables a favorable trade or immediate KO.
  • Switching would expose a teammate to worse risk than staying in.

Move Selecting

  • De-prioritizes moves that don't connect or are invalidated by known immunities.
  • De-prioritizes setup/status when it doesn't change the turn outcome versus immediate pressure.
  • Ignores utility if a reliable KO is available right now.
Taunt
  • Favored versus status-leaning or setup-leaning targets.
  • If a clean KO is available, it typically chooses the KO line instead of Taunt.
  • Versus pure offense targets, Taunt is often de-prioritized.
Pivot moves (U-turn / Volt Switch / Flip Turn)
  • Hit-and-switch moves are treated as strategic tools. When pivoting creates value (momentum, positioning, form/condition enabling), the AI tends to favor it.
Knock Off
  • If the target still has a meaningful removable item, the AI tends to prefer Knock Off.
  • Once item pressure is no longer relevant, it reverts to the more efficient damage/utility choice.
Protect Effect
  • For sets that benefit from early activation via held items, the AI can use Protect as an immediate setup line to maximize pressure next turn.
Shed Tail

When it's used

  • When the pivot is relatively safe and a strong beneficiary exists on the bench.
  • When the resulting entry produces clear advantage (momentum / positioning).

When it's avoided

  • When HP is too low (line becomes too risky).
  • When no useful/meaningful follow-up switch exists.

Battle knowledge

  • The AI may still attempt a risky line against an ability that hasn't been confirmed yet.
  • After witnessing an immunity/absorb interaction, it will have memory of it.

Illusion interaction

  • Initially, the AI can be misled by the apparent typing.
  • After incompatible signals, it corrects its read and remember the ability.
  • If the AI is using Illusion itself, move choice follows the Pokémon's real typing.

Double battles behavior

In doubles, the AI reasons in a coordinated way: it evaluates both opponents and partner interactions, then selects the target + move pair with the highest practical value for the turn.

What it evaluates each turn

  • Scores each move against each valid target, then chooses the strongest target/move combination.
  • Reads partner intent (or last known ally move) to avoid wasting both actions on redundant lines.
  • Penalizes support moves if the partner cannot convert that support into pressure.

How field Pokémon influence each other

  • Avoids careless spread damage into ally unless the trade is clearly favorable.
  • Can intentionally interact with ally abilities/items (immunity absorbs, policy triggers, etc.) when profitable.
  • Reduces duplicated utility in the same turn (e.g. overlapping weather/status support).
Helping Hand
  • Used when the partner has an immediate damaging conversion, especially when the boost improves the turn outcome (for example from non-KO into KO).
  • De-prioritized if the partner has no damage move, is switching, or is already on a non-converting status line.
  • If Helping Hand does not improve the immediate board state, the AI may choose another useful action.
Fake Out
  • Strongly tied to first turn in field: outside first turn, it is effectively discarded.
  • On first turn it is favored when flinch has real value or when it is still the best immediate damage line.
  • It is avoided into known anti-flinch / anti-secondary contexts (e.g. Covert Cloak, Inner Focus, Shield Dust, Substitute).

Notes

  • Results come from targeted scenarios: great for isolating decisions, not exhaustive for long multi-turn battles.
  • Not every advanced multi-turn interaction has been covered yet.
  • A small probabilistic component may occasionally produce different choices in seemingly identical states.

Competitive tip: versus Bosses, rely less on surprise and more on robust lines that remain strong after reveal.
