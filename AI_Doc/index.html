1. How the AI picks a move

Every turn, each enemy Pokémon gives a score to each of its moves. Every move starts at 100 points. The AI then goes through a fixed series of checks that add or remove points. The move with the highest final score is used. If two or more moves tie, one of them is picked at random.

  • Bonuses are small, usually between +1 and +6. A few points decide the move.
  • Penalties are large (−10, −20, −30). They push a pointless move far below any sensible option.
  • A move's score never goes below 0. Moves with no PP or that can't be selected (Disable, Taunt, Choice lock, etc.) are ignored.
  • Before it scores its moves, the AI decides whether to switch out. A switch always replaces the attack.

In this manual, the target is the player's Pokémon the AI is facing.

The AI uses the middle damage roll (not the highest or lowest) when checking KO. A KO is called guaranteed only when even the lowest roll is enough.

2. Standard trainers vs Bosses

All trainers use the same thinking core and the same set of behaviours. The difference between a normal trainer and a Boss is information.

Standard Route trainers and Grunts

  • Always sees species, level, types, stats, current HP, stat stages and status of the target.
  • Knows only the moves already used in this battle.
  • Knows the target's ability only after it has been revealed. Until then it guesses one of the species' possible abilities, and the guess can change every turn.
  • Knows the held item only after it has been revealed (activated, knocked off, Frisked, etc.).
  • Before any attack is seen, it guesses whether the target is a physical or special attacker from base stats.

Boss Level Cap Trainers: Gym Leaders, Rivals, etc.

  • Knows everything from the start of the battle: moves, ability and held item of every Pokémon in the player's party, including those still in the back.
  • All Gym Leaders and the Champion are Bosses, along with rival battles and the story battles that unlock a new level cap (e.g. the grunts in Petalburg Woods and Rusturf Tunnel, Maxie in the Desert). A few special trainers are Bosses too.
  • A Boss reasons with real data from turn 1. Its choices don't get "better" as the battle reveals information, because there is nothing left to reveal.

Shared by every trainer

  • Memory: whatever a Standard trainer learns about a Pokémon is remembered for the whole battle, even after it switches out and back in.
  • Last move read: the AI looks at the target's last used move to judge Protect, Taunt, Encore, Counter, Sucker Punch and turn order. Nobody predicts the next move or a switch.
  • Own team: the AI always knows its own moves, abilities and items perfectly.
  • Illusion: a disguised Pokémon fools every trainer, Bosses included. The AI sees through it once Illusion breaks or is revealed, or once the disguised Pokémon uses a move the fake species can't learn. When the AI itself has Illusion, it plays according to its real typing.

3. The AI's thinking

Every turn, each AI Pokémon goes through these steps:

  1. Read the board Calculates the damage of each of its moves against the target and of the target's known moves against it, works out who moves first (priority included) and how many hits each side needs to KO.
  2. Check switching Finds the best party member to bring in right now (see Which Pokémon it sends in), then goes through the switch rules in order. The first rule that fires decides the switch. Many rules only fire if a good switch-in exists.
  3. Score every move, in this order: ① Bad move filter (penalties for moves that fail or are pointless) → ② KO bonus → ③ best attack bonus + effect bonuses → ④ HP awareness → ⑤ partner logic (double battles only).
  4. Final switch check If every move scored 90 or less, the AI switches out (when it can). If a pivot move (U-turn, Volt Switch…) or Revival Blessing scored above 100, it cancels a planned switch and uses that move instead.
  5. Act Uses the highest-scoring move (random among ties), or switches.

6. Step 2: KO logic, speed and priority

Situation (damaging moves only)Score
The move KOs the target and the AI moves first ("fast kill")+6
The move KOs the target but the target moves first ("slow kill")+4
…and the KO is guaranteed even on the lowest damage roll+1 extra
The target can KO the AI, the AI doesn't outspeed, and this move has priority ("last chance")+2
Explosion-type moves never get the KO bonusn/a

How "who moves first" is decided

  • Priority first: the AI compares the priority of the move it is scoring with the priority of the target's last used move. After a priority move, the AI expects it again.
  • Speed: real Speed including stat stages, paralysis, Choice Scarf, Tailwind, weather abilities, etc. Trick Room reverses the order. Lagging Tail and Stall always move last.
  • Speed ties count as the AI moving first.

What a KO check takes into account

  • Focus Sash, Sturdy and Disguise at full HP: a single hit is not counted as a KO (multi-hit moves still are). A Standard trainer only accounts for a Focus Sash once it has been revealed.
  • Multi-hit moves, Parental Bond, Skill Link and similar effects are counted with their real number of hits. Combo Punch Smeragis is counted as one hit per arm of the user.
  • Critical hits are not counted.

Once the target is in KO range, expect the AI to go for the kill. A fast KO adds +6 to +9 points once the other bonuses are counted, more than almost any utility move can earn. The main exceptions are moves the AI scores very highly, such as screens or hazards on its first turn (+4), but those almost never beat a clean KO.

7. Step 3a: Choosing between attacks

All trainers prefer their highest-damage move.

  • The single strongest attack (highest expected damage against the target) gets +1.
  • Every other attack can also get +1, but only if it needs no more hits to KO than any other attack and isn't worse on the tie-breakers below. When two attacks would both 2HKO, the more accurate one can tie with the strongest one.
  • Tie-breakers between attacks that need the same number of hits: ① no charging turn (Solar Beam, Meteor Beam…) ② higher accuracy ③ better side effect, and no contact into Rocky Helmet / Rough Skin / Iron Barbs.
  • An attack that deals no damage at all to the target (an immunity the AI knows about, Wonder Guard, etc.) gets −20 and nothing else is checked.

8. Step 3b: Move effect scores

After the damage check, every move (status or damaging) gets bonuses for its effect. Secondary effects of damaging moves count only when they are guaranteed to happen (e.g. Close Combat's stat drop, Icy Wind's Speed drop, Knock Off). A 10% burn chance is ignored.

Pivoting

U-turn, Volt Switch, Flip Turn, Parting Shot, Chilly Reception (singles):

  • +4 (should pivot) when: the AI moves first and can't KO the target, but the target can KO it; or it wants to break a Focus Sash / Sturdy / Multiscale at full HP; or it already wanted to switch out; or it moves second, can't KO the target and would be 2HKO'd.

9. Step 4: HP awareness

The last scoring pass looks at both Pokémon's HP and adjusts the score.

AI's own HP

  • Above 70%: −2 to healing moves, Rest, Explosion, Destiny Bond, Flail/Reversal, Endure, Memento, Grudge. No need yet.
  • 30–70%: −2 to stat-changing status moves, screens, Safeguard, Mist, Focus Energy, Belly Drum, Fillet Away, Explosion.
  • Below 30%: −2 to stat-changing moves, screens, weather moves, Belly Drum, Psych Up, Mirror Coat, Tickle and similar long-term plays.

Target's HP

  • The move KOs the target: +2.
  • Above 70%: no change.
  • 30–70%: −2 to setup moves, stat drops, Pain Split, Perish Song, Safeguard, Tickle and poison moves.
  • Below 30%: −2 to every status move. The AI just wants to finish it.

11. When the AI switches out

Standard trainers and Bosses use the same switching rules. The AI checks the rules below in this order and switches on the first one that fires. The % is the chance it actually switches when the rule applies.

It never switches if…

  • it is trapped (Mean Look, Shadow Tag, Arena Trap, Magnet Pull, binding moves, Ingrain…);
  • it has no other Pokémon able to come in;
  • the outgoing Pokémon would later be KO'd by entry hazards on re-entry and no teammate can clear them (this blocks most rules below).

• the target OHKOs it and outspeeds (or OHKOs it while it can't even deal 50% back), while it has 50%+ HP (25%+ with Regenerator), or
• the target's types hit it super effectively, it has no super-effective move, it has 50%+ HP, and it has no important status move (hazards, screens, Toxic, sleep, paralysis, burn, Hoarfrost Smeragis, Leech Seed, Trick, Trick Room, Fake Out…).		50%
10Encore: locked into a status move: 100%. Locked into an attack that isn't super effective: 50%. Stays if the locked move is super effective.see rule
11Bad Choice lock: locked into a status move or a move that doesn't affect the target.100%
12Attacking stat lowered (its main attacking stat): −2 → 50% · −3 or worse → 100%.see rule
13All moves are bad: after scoring, every move is at 90 or less.100%

A planned switch is cancelled if a pivot move or Revival Blessing scores above 100. The AI then uses that move to get out (or revive) instead.

12. Which Pokémon it sends in

In single battles every trainer chooses its next Pokémon by simulating a 1v1 against the target. It counts how many hits each side needs, who moves first, and the damage it would take from all the target's moves.

"Can it win the 1v1?"

  • After a KO (a free switch): the candidate must survive more hits than it needs to KO the target, or the same number while moving first.
  • Mid-battle switch (it takes a hit coming in): it needs one hit more of margin, or the same margin while moving first.
  • If the target can't damage it at all, it wins. If it can't damage the target, it loses.

After one of its Pokémon faints

  1. A trapper that wins the 1v1
  2. A faster OHKO (revenge killer)
  3. A slower OHKO that still wins the 1v1
  4. A faster 2HKO, then a slower 2HKO
  5. Best resistance to the target's types + a super-effective move
  6. Best resistance to the target's types
  7. A Baton Pass user that survives long enough
  8. Any Pokémon that wins the 1v1
  9. The one dealing the most damage (if it isn't OHKO'd)
  10. None of the above: the last available Pokémon in party order

Steps 3 to 9 also require the candidate to win the 1v1.

When it switches out voluntarily

  1. A trapper that wins the 1v1
  2. Best resistance + super-effective move
  3. Best resistance
  4. The bulkiest option (survives 4+ of the target's hits)
  5. A Baton Pass user
  6. Any Pokémon that wins the 1v1

If no candidate qualifies, most switch rules (bad odds, status, Encore…) don't fire at all. The AI stays in and fights.

A Pokémon with Truant is never sent in while it would be stuck loafing against the target.

13. Double battles

Choosing move and target

  • Each AI Pokémon scores every move against every possible target (both opposing Pokémon and its own partner) and uses the highest move/target pair.
  • A move aimed at its own partner is only considered if it scores 100 or more (e.g. Heal Pulse, or an Electric move into a Volt Absorb partner).
  • The second AI Pokémon knows what its partner has chosen this turn and plans around it.

Spread moves (Earthquake, Surf, Discharge…)

  • If the move would KO its own partner: +10 if it also KOs both opposing Pokémon. Small bonus if the partner is doomed anyway (and more with Moxie, Receiver or Last Respects). −2 if it only KOs one opponent. Otherwise −10.
  • If the partner survives, the friendly fire is accepted. The AI may still prefer a single-target attack when the partner would take heavy damage.
  • Deliberately hitting the partner to trigger its Weakness Policy only happens if the partner can take at least 4 such hits.

Protect, Fake Out and support in doubles

  • Protect is penalised harder when repeated: a second use in a row has a 50% chance of −10, and every use in a row costs −2 more.
  • Fake Out only works on the user's first turn on the field and is avoided into Covert Cloak, Inner Focus, Shield Dust or a Substitute.
  • Helping Hand is used when the partner is attacking. Never when the partner is switching or using a status move.
  • Pivot moves get no "should pivot" bonus in doubles. They are judged as attacks.

Switching in doubles

  • The "bad odds" and "free turn" rules (#4 and #9) are not used in doubles. The others still apply.
  • Send-ins use a simpler method: first a Baton Pass user, then the Pokémon whose typing best resists the opposing Pokémon and has a super-effective move, then the one dealing the most damage. If nothing fits, the last Pokémon in the party.

14. Other behaviour

  • Mega Evolution / Terastallization: enemy Pokémon set up for it Mega Evolve or Terastallize the first time they can act.
  • Bag items: enemy trainers don't use bag items during battle.
  • Self-KO moves: trainers never actively look for Explosion / Self-Destruct / Final Gambit / Memento plays.
  • Wild Pokémon: normal wild Pokémon choose moves at random. Some scripted wild encounters use the trainer AI described here.
  • In-game partners: an AI partner fighting alongside the player uses the same logic.

Random aspects

  • Ties between equal scores are broken at random.
  • First Protect when the target didn't just attack: +1 about 39% of the time. Second Protect in a row: 50% chance of −6.
  • Speed-lowering status moves: +2 about 73% of the time.
  • Healing when not in danger: encouraged 2 times out of 3.
  • Standard trainers re-guess an unrevealed ability every turn.
  • Most switch rules have a % chance (see the switch table).
